## Should You Read This?

**Toby's Rating: 9/10**

[Dare to Lead](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0399592520) by Brené Brown is the communication book hiding inside a leadership book. Rumbling with vulnerability. Living your values out loud. BRAVING trust.

If you’ve already read [Atlas of the Heart](/post/atlas-of-the-heart-summary), this is the one that takes the emotion vocabulary to work. If you haven’t, you can start here. The sentence that earns the whole book:

> Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind.

That’s [Radical Candor](/post/summary-radical-candor-by-kim-scott) in six words. It’s also the opposite of how I still behave when I’m tired.

## Toby's Top Takeaway From Dare to Lead by Brené Brown

I still choose unclear because it feels kinder.

I delay the underperformance conversation. I wait. I coach around the edges. I write a fuzzy email that could mean three things. I tell myself I’m protecting them.

I’m protecting me.

Brown calls it armour. Perfectionism, cynicism, the joke that keeps the room from getting real. My armour is optimism. I believe people can turn it around. Often they can. Sometimes the kindest sentence is the clear one, six months earlier.

Clear is kind is now a test I run on my own drafts.

- Is the request specific?
- Is the standard visible?
- Have I said the thing I’m hoping they’ll infer?

If the answer is no, I’m being unkind. I’m also wasting their time. They can’t meet a standard I won’t speak.

The other idea I use in almost every leadership workshop is the marble jar.

Trust is not a grand gesture. It’s a marble in, marble out. You keep a confidence. Marble in. You talk about them in a meeting they weren’t in. Marble out. You show up for the boring one-to-one. Marble in. You cancel it three times. Jar empty.

Organisations try to buy trust with offsites. The jar is filled on Tuesdays at 9.15.

BRAVING is her checklist. Boundaries. Reliability. Accountability. Vault. Integrity. Non-judgement. Generosity. I’ve put it on a slide. The slide does nothing. Asking “which of these did we break?” after a messy conversation does something.

Dare to Lead can get a bit slogan-heavy. There are workbooks. I still rate it a 9 because it gave language to the thing my other conversation books assume: you won’t use the playbook if you’re hiding.

[Fierce Conversations](/post/summary-fierce-conversations-by-susan-scott) tells you to come out from behind yourself. Brown tells you why you went back there.

## 3 Big Ideas From Dare to Lead by Brené Brown

**1. Vulnerability is staying in the room**

Brown’s rumble is not a sharing circle. It’s a committed talk about something uncomfortable, with no guarantee you’ll look good.

Leaders skip it. We jump to the plan. We skip the “I’m scared this programme will make people feel stupid.” Then the programme dies in sarcasm.

Rumbling sounds like:

- “The story I’m making up is…”
- “I need to tell you I’m stuck.”
- “I don’t have the answer and I need your help.”

Vulnerability is not oversharing your childhood in the Monday stand-up. It’s staying when the outcome isn’t controlled. A restructure. A missed number. An AI rollout that will change someone’s job.

**2. Values you can’t operationalise are posters**

Brown wants two values, not twelve. Then behaviours. What does courage look like in a budget meeting? What does it look like when we give feedback?

If the value is respect and we still send the late-night pile-on, the value is a lie. Teams know. They always know.

Write the behaviours as conversation rules. How we disagree. How we say no. How we talk about people who aren’t in the room.

**3. Learn to recover or you’ll stop having the conversation**

The conversation will go badly. You’ll over-explain. They’ll get upset. You’ll send the email you should have slept on.

If you have no way back, you’ll avoid the next one. Not because you lacked a model. Because you didn’t know how to recover.

Brown’s version: feel it, check the story, repair, try again.

[The Next Conversation](/post/summary-the-next-conversation-by-jefferson-fisher) is the tactical version of that hope. Your next one can be different from your last.
