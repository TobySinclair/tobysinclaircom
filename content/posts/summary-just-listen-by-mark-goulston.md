## Should You Read This?

**Toby's Rating: 8/10**

[Just Listen](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0814436471) by Mark Goulston is the most tactical listening book I have read that is not written by a coach. Goulston is a psychiatrist who worked with FBI hostage negotiators and with executives who cannot get through to a colleague, a customer, or their own teenager.

It sits beside [You’re Not Listening](/post/book-summary-you-re-not-listening-by-kate-murphy-big-ideas-and-best-quotes) and [How to Listen](/post/summary-how-to-listen-by-oscar-trimboli). Murphy and Trimboli will make you wiser. Goulston will give you a move for the person who is already in amygdala.

ISBN: 9780814436479

## Toby's Top Takeaway From Just Listen by Mark Goulston

They did not want my plan.

They wanted to feel that I had got it.

I am slow to learn this. I am a coach and I still reach for the fix. Someone is angry about a reorg. I sketch a stakeholder map. Someone is scared of AI replacing their craft. I talk about upskilling. Useful later. First they need to feel felt.

Goulston’s phrase is exactly that. **Feeling felt.** Mirror neurons. The other person’s nervous system registers that you are with them. Until that happens, your advice is noise.

The tiniest tool in the book is the one I use most.

**“Oh?”**

Said with warmth. Not sarcasm. It invites the next layer. I have watched people who were performing a position drop into the real problem after two “oh?”s and a pause. It is almost unfair.

The other move I have borrowed for underperformance conversations — the ones I still delay:

“What are you most worried I am going to say?”
Then later: “What do you need from me right now?”

I have spent years skating around poor performance because I am optimistic. I coach. I wait. I hope. [Crucial Conversations](/post/summary-crucial-conversations-by-kerry-patterson) already caught me at that. Goulston adds the emotional on-ramp. If they are defensive, you cannot STATE your path yet. You have to get them from reptile brain back to a place where words work.

He uses a simple ladder. From resistant, to listening, to willing, to invested. Most leaders start at invested — “here is the plan” — and then act surprised at resistance.

My early career was the opposite failure. I went in hot. Email. Detail. The other person cried. I had not made them feel felt. I had made them feel judged. Same result as silence: no change.

Just Listen is a little American-self-help in places. Some of the stories are neat. I still keep it because the techniques are stealable on a Tuesday.

## 3 Big Ideas From Just Listen by Mark Goulston

**Big Idea 1 — Reach the emotions first or you will not reach the person**

When someone is angry, scared, or humiliated, they cannot take your logic. You are speaking the wrong language.

Goulston’s sequence: make them feel heard, then felt, then ready. Only then problem-solve.

This is why “calm down” never works. It is an evaluation of their state. [Nonviolent Communication](/post/summary-nonviolent-communication-by-marshall-rosenberg) would translate it into a need. Goulston would have you name what you are seeing. “You look furious. I would be too.” Then shut up.

I use a version in Real Talk Studio rehearsals. We do not start with the script. We start with the body. If your voice is already in a fight, the script will not save you. [Jefferson Fisher](/post/summary-the-next-conversation-by-jefferson-fisher) would begin with the breath. Same science. Different accent.

**Big Idea 2 — Questions that unlock the stuck person**

A few of Goulston’s questions have earned a place next to Stanier’s coaching seven:

- The impossibility question. “What would need to happen for this to feel solvable?”
- The magic paradox. Agree with the part of their resistance that is true, then ask for the exception.
- “What do you need me to understand that I am not getting?”
- “On a scale of one to ten, how ready are you to talk about this? What would make it an eight?”

These are not tricks. They return agency. Advice removes it.

If you already like [Ask](/post/summary-ask-by-jeff-wetzler), you will recognise the pattern. Safety, then a real question, then listen to learn. Goulston is simply more clinical about the flooded brain.

**Big Idea 3 — The most powerful people are often the most starved of being listened to**

I used to think senior leaders did not need this. They have airtime. They have status.

They are often the least listened to as humans. People bring them performances. Nobody says “you look tired.” Goulston is good on this. Get through to the person, not the role.

It works down the hierarchy too. The quiet engineer is not shy. They have learned that speaking is expensive. [Humble Inquiry](/post/summary-humble-inquiry-by-edgar-schein) builds the climate. Just Listen is what you do in the chair when they finally risk a sentence.

Do not waste that sentence by topping it.

## Best Quotes From Just Listen by Mark Goulston

> “The more you can get them to talk, the more they will feel you understand them. And the more they feel you understand them, the more they will be willing to hear you.”

> “Feeling felt is the moment the other person knows you get them. Until then, you are just talking.”

> “If you want to get through to people, you have to go to where they are, not where you want them to be.”

> “When people are upset, they want to know that you understand how they feel more than they want you to fix it.”

> “Oh?”

> “Ask questions that begin with what and how. Why makes people defend.”
