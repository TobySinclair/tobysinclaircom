## **Artificial Intelligence Coaching:  Myth or Miracle?**

AI and Coaching might be two of the most overloaded terms on the planet. Combined, they can be a recipe for confusion and misunderstanding. Therefore, defining terms is an important place to start.

AI, broadly defined, leverages computer systems to simulate human intelligence processes, including learning, reasoning, and self-correction. In 2023 AI hit the headlines due to the advancements in AI-powered Chat Bots. Predominantly the advancements made by OpenAI, creators of ChatGPT. These use a type of AI called generative AI.

Coaching might be even trickier to define than AI. Let's take a very [formal definition](https://coachingfederation.org/credentials-and-standards/core-competencies) from the International Coaching Federation:

> Partnering with clients in a thought-provoking and creative process that inspires them to maximize their personal and professional potential, which is particularly important in today’s uncertain and complex environment. Coaches honour the client as the expert in his or her life and work and believe every client is creative, resourceful and whole.

However, this formal definition is not how the general public would describe it. When a friend said they worked as a coach, the person responded: “Which sport?”. Coaching is associated with experts advising to help people solve problems. In the workplace, it is typically associated with fixing underperformance: “I need a coach to fix my team”.

You didn’t come here for coaching definitions, but this contrasting definition is important. When you find an AI coach, the style of coaching will vary. Without training and guidance, tools will very much follow the [dictionary definition of coaching](https://www.tobysinclair.com/post/coaching-definition).

I asked ChatGPT to define coaching in a one-sentence summary:

> “Coaching is a collaborative process in which a trained professional (coach) helps individuals or groups achieve their goals, maximize their potential, and develop personally or professionally through guidance, support, and accountability.”

*- ChatGPT 3.5*

Nothing groundbreaking, but one word does stand out; **guidance**. This typically means giving advice and direction. So out-of-the-box AI coaches will often take a directive approach. Professional coaches should not be surprised to find most AI coaches are not coaching as per the ICF competencies.

Even if the coaching style of the AI does align there are also technical constraints that limit AI coaching effectiveness. Jazz Rasool has an [excellent article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/spectrums-coaching-jazz-rasool-q8vpe/) that defines coaching on a spectrum from Transactional to Transformational.

Transactional coaching focuses on specific tasks or objectives, offering immediate solutions and feedback. Transformational coaching dives deeper, aiming for profound changes in beliefs, values, and systems of thought over time. [Today](http://time.Today)'s AI coaching platforms and tools predominantly deliver transactional conversations. Helping clients work through problems in a very logical and rational approach. Typically they implement a simple coaching model such as GROW. They step through a series of steps/questions to help the client think through their challenge.

Based on this, some professional coaches may quickly discount AI coaching tools. Claiming that they cannot possibly help create the transformational outcomes that coaching conversations enable. Whilst I do agree with this reservation, it is missing other opportunities. Transactional coaching, although simpler, can still deliver good outcomes for clients. A simple question or just spending a short amount of time with a thinking partner, albeit a machine, can lead to good outcomes. Later on in this guide, I share how Transformational coaching might very well be possible in the future.

Despite the limited capabilities of AI today, several professional coaches are embracing this future. Their mission is to build AI coaches that coach in line with professional competencies. Some even hope, including myself, that we can build an AI coach in the future that can provide transformational coaching conversations.

Still not convinced? Here are some real-life examples of how AI coaching is being used today.

## **Challenges with AI Coaching for Clients and Business Owners**

While AI coaching presents numerous opportunities for growth and impact in the coaching industry, it also faces significant challenges and limitations, particularly in the realms of emotional connection and privacy.

**Loss of Human Touch and Emotional Connection**

The essence of coaching lies in the unique, empathetic relationship built between coach and client, a connection grounded in mutual understanding and trust. Coaches not only offer skilled listening but also emotional support, which is paramount during times of personal or professional turmoil. AI, despite its advancements, cannot fully comprehend and replicate human emotions. It may provide insights based on data but cannot replace the nuanced understanding a human coach brings to a session. This limitation raises concerns about AI's effectiveness in scenarios that require deep emotional intelligence and empathy. The absence of genuine human interaction could leave clients feeling underserved, particularly those navigating complex emotional challenges. Despite this challenge, I’m reminded that not every coaching conversation needs to be a transformative conversation that leads to a life-changing result. Additionally, most AI coaching tools are text-based, which could limit the depth of conversation. While some platforms are exploring voice capabilities to enrich interactions, the challenge of ensuring these conversations remain private and secure persists.

**Privacy, Security, and Ethical Considerations**

As with any technology that handles personal data, AI coaching introduces privacy and security concerns. Clients entrust coaches with sensitive information, expecting confidentiality. AI systems, especially those relying on cloud storage and processing, are vulnerable to data breaches, potentially compromising client privacy. Moreover, the ethical use of AI in coaching demands rigorous standards to ensure that algorithms are free from biases and that decisions made by AI are transparent and explainable. Safeguarding against these risks is paramount to maintaining client trust.

**Safety and Well-being**

Sometimes coaching clients may share distressing or traumatic experiences. The ability of AI to recognize these situations and appropriately signpost resources or escalate to human intervention is crucial. The current capabilities of AI may not always accurately identify or respond to such scenarios, highlighting the need for safeguards and human oversight in AI coaching solutions.

Addressing these challenges is crucial for the sustainable integration of AI into coaching practices. As technology evolves, finding solutions that preserve the core values of coaching while leveraging AI's strengths will be key to enhancing the coaching industry.

## **What Makes a “Good” AI Coach?**

Creating a "Good" AI coach involves a blend of advanced technology, psychological insight, and user-friendly design. Here are five considerations when developing an AI Coach:

1. **Personalization Capabilities:** A good AI coach should offer a high level of personalization, adapting its coaching style and content based on individual user needs and goals. A reminder, ICF defines coaching as a “thought-provoking and creative process” so a conversation with an AI coach should feel this way too.
2. **Evidence-based Techniques:** For professional coaches, it's crucial that the AI coach coaches. You should review the AI coach against the ICF coaching competencies. For example, does the AI coach stay in a coaching stance, or move to giving advice and solving the problem for the client? As we explore at the start of this guide, without training, most AI coaches will follow the dictionary definition of coaching and give lots of advice.
3. **User Privacy and Data Security:** Given the personal nature of coaching, the AI platform must have robust privacy policies and data protection measures in place. These should be transparent to the client and easy to understand. Over time, this will no doubt become an important factor in clients choosing AI coaches they can trust.
4. **User Interface and Experience**: The platform should be intuitive and user-friendly, making it easy for users of all tech-savviness levels to benefit from the coaching provided. ChatGPT provides both text and voice interaction. In the future, no doubt there will be further integration with Virtual Reality platforms.
5. **Integration with Other Tools:** The ability to integrate with other productivity tools and platforms can enhance the coaching experience, allowing for a more holistic development approach. A simple example is integration with calendar tools for scheduling and reminders.

## **Red Flags to Watch Out For**

I’ve referenced several times the wide variation in AI coaches. When working with an AI coach or service provider, there are a few red flags to watch for:

1. **Overreliance on Generic Responses:** If an AI coach often provides generic, one-size-fits-all advice, it may not be sophisticated enough to truly personalize the coaching experience. In this case, likely, the prompt used for the coaching is poorly developed, and/or they are using a lower-power AI model, such as GPT3.5 vs GPT4.
2. **Lack of Transparency About Data Use**: Providers that are not clear about how they use and protect user data could pose a privacy risk.
3. **Lack of Coaching Session History:** AI coaches should ideally learn and evolve based on user interactions. A lack of this feature could mean the coaching will not improve or adapt over time. It will create a disjointed experience for the client.

Developing an AI coach for your business requires careful consideration of these factors to ensure that it will genuinely benefit the client, providing meaningful, personalized conversation while maintaining privacy and security.
